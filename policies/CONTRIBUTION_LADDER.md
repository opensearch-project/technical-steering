## Overview

The OpenSearch Project is open to all. No formal role is required to participate and be part of the community. This contribution ladder serves as a guide for contributors interested in taking on a larger role within the project, up to and including becoming a maintainer.

Roles are not permanent. Contributors step up and step back as their circumstances change, and maintainers who move on are recognized as [Emeritus Maintainers](#emeritus-maintainer).

## The Ladder

| Role | Responsibilities | Requirements | Defined by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | Comment on issues and pull requests, review pull requests, create pull requests, create issues | None | GitHub account |
| Member | Active contributor: triage issues, manage labels, assign issues | Demonstrated activity, plus sponsorship from a maintainer | opensearch-project GitHub organization membership |
| Maintainer | Approve and merge pull requests, set technical direction within a repository | Each repo models its own definition of the required body of work, see [RESPONSIBILITIES.md](RESPONSIBILITIES.md) | Entry in the repository's MAINTAINERS.md file |

Former maintainers are recognized as [Emeritus Maintainers](#emeritus-maintainer).

## Public

Anyone with a GitHub account can participate in the OpenSearch Project:

- Create and comment on issues.
- Create pull requests and review pull requests submitted by others.
- Participate in discussions on the [forum](https://forum.opensearch.org/) and in [community meetings](https://www.meetup.com/opensearch/).

No approval or process is required. Contributors at this level are expected to follow the [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/opensearch-project/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and the [contribution guidelines](https://github.com/opensearch-project/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Member

Members are active contributors who have been granted membership in the [opensearch-project](https://github.com/opensearch-project) GitHub organization. Membership recognizes sustained participation and grants tooling access that makes ongoing contribution easier.

### Requirements

- **Demonstrated activity** in one or more repositories of the organization. Examples include merged pull requests, high-quality issue reports and reproductions, code reviews, participation in issue triage, and documentation improvements. There is no fixed quota; the body of work should show sustained, constructive engagement rather than a single contribution.
- **Sponsorship from one maintainer** of a repository the candidate has contributed to. The sponsor vouches for the candidate's contributions and adherence to the [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/opensearch-project/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

### Privileges

- Membership in the opensearch-project GitHub organization.
- Triage-level access for issue management: applying and managing labels, assigning issues, and marking duplicates.
- Eligibility to have issues assigned to them.

### Becoming a Member

1. The candidate opens an issue in the [.github](https://github.com/opensearch-project/.github) repository using the organization membership request template, listing their contributions (links to merged pull requests, reviews, triage participation, etc.) and naming their sponsor.
2. The sponsoring maintainer confirms the sponsorship by commenting (+1) on the issue.
3. If the requirements are met, the [members of the admin team](https://github.com/opensearch-project/.github/blob/main/ADMINS.md) invite the candidate to the GitHub organization, add them to the [triage team](https://github.com/orgs/opensearch-project/teams/triage) and close the issue.

If a request is declined, the admin team notes the reason on the issue. Candidates are welcome to reapply after addressing the feedback.

### Removing a Member

Removal of members, whether by choice, due to inactivity, or for actions that negatively impact the project, follows the same policy as [Removing a Maintainer](RESPONSIBILITIES.md#removing-a-maintainer). Any past member can be reinstated through the [Becoming a Member](#becoming-a-member) process, and prior contributions count toward the activity requirement. There is no "emeritus" status for members.

## Maintainer

Maintainers approve and merge pull requests, and set technical direction within a repository. Maintainer status is granted per repository and recorded in that repository's MAINTAINERS.md file.

The maintainer role is defined in [RESPONSIBILITIES.md](RESPONSIBILITIES.md). In contrast to membership, time and a sustained body of work are factors: each repository models its own definition of what it takes to become a maintainer, and past nominations in the repository's MAINTAINERS.md history give a good sense of the expected body of work.

Member status is the natural stepping stone to maintainership but is not a formal prerequisite. Maintainer nomination follows the process in [RESPONSIBILITIES.md](RESPONSIBILITIES.md#becoming-a-maintainer) regardless of whether the nominee is an organization member.

## Emeritus Maintainer

Stepping back is a normal and expected part of the maintainer lifecycle. Maintainers who are no longer active on a repository are recognized in the "Emeritus" section of the repository's MAINTAINERS.md file. See [moving on](RESPONSIBILITIES.md#moving-on) for more detail.
